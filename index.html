The ten skills a model can't file for you

AI compresses execution — searching, summarizing, first-draft claim language. What's left standing is judgment under ambiguity, multi-audience reasoning (examiner, competitor, judge, investor), and accountability. Below, Claim 1 is the broadest and most foundational; Claims 2–10 depend from it, each adding a narrower commercial capability.

1

Patent Claims Architecture & Strategic Scope Engineering

Translating a live, changing system — an agentic pipeline, a multi-modal model, a distributed architecture — into claim language that is simultaneously broad enough to matter, narrow enough to survive prior art, and durable enough to hold up in litigation years later.

Why it survives: requires holding one technical mental model while simulating three adversarial readers (examiner, infringer's counsel, judge) at once — a sustained, stateful, multi-perspective judgment task, not a retrieval or summarization task.
2
Wherein Claim 1 further comprises —

Freedom-to-Operate & Design-Around Strategy

Not the clearance search itself (AI does that well now) — the judgment call on which cleared risks to accept, engineer around, license, or fight, made under real commercial deadlines.

Why it survives: the output is a risk-tolerance decision tied to a specific business's appetite, not a factual answer.
3
Wherein Claim 1 further comprises —

IP Portfolio Valuation, Monetization & Deal Structuring

Turning a stack of patents into a negotiable number — royalty stacking, M&A diligence pricing, IP-backed financing, licensing leverage.

Why it survives: valuation is a negotiation position, not a formula; the "right" number depends on who's across the table.
4
Wherein Claim 1 further comprises —

Patent Landscape Mining & Whitespace Intelligence

Pattern-finding across thousands of claims to locate genuinely open, defensible ground — the strategic layer above a prior-art search.

Why it survives: whitespace is a judgment about commercial relevance, not just claim-language absence.
5
Wherein Claim 1 further comprises —

AI/Algorithm-Specific Eligibility Engineering (§101)

Architecting claims and specifications so software/AI inventions survive the Alice/Mayo framework and the USPTO's fast-moving 2024–2025 AI eligibility guidance.

Why it survives: the doctrine itself is a moving target — durable skill is tracking and re-engineering around it, not memorizing today's rule.
6
Wherein Claim 1 further comprises —

Standard-Essential Patents (SEP) & FRAND Strategy

Positioning claims and portfolios inside evolving technical standards — 5G/6G, AI-model interoperability, codecs — for negotiating leverage.

Why it survives: standards politics and FRAND valuation disputes require reading committee dynamics, not just claim text.
7
Wherein Claim 1 further comprises —

Trade Secret vs. Patent Strategic Election

Deciding invention-by-invention whether disclosure (patent) or secrecy (trade secret) protects more value — increasingly sharp for training data, model weights, and inference pipelines.

Why it survives: an irreversible bet made under incomplete information about a competitor's future moves.
8
Wherein Claim 1 further comprises —

Cross-Border Filing & Global Prosecution Strategy

Sequencing PCT and national-phase filings, budget, and claim-style adjustments across USPTO, EPO, CNIPA, and JPO practice.

Why it survives: requires live coordination across jurisdictions with different examiners, timelines, and incentives.
9
Wherein Claim 1 further comprises —

IP Due Diligence for M&A / VC Transactions

Auditing a target's IP stack for enforceability gaps, inventorship defects, and encumbrances — under a deal clock, not an academic one.

Why it survives: the deliverable is a go/no-go judgment with real money and real deadlines attached.
10
Wherein Claim 1 further comprises —

Litigation-Informed Drafting

Writing today's claims with tomorrow's Markman hearing already in mind — prosecution history estoppel, claim construction foresight, doctrine-of-equivalents preservation.

Why it survives: requires forecasting how a court will misread your own words in an adversarial context years from now.

How to use this curriculum

Work module by module, 0 through 4. Each subheading in the Structure tab has a matching entry in Assignments — do the reading (Resources), then complete the assignment before moving on. Module 4 ends in a single capstone claim set.
